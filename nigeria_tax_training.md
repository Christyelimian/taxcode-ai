## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

On June 26, 2025, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu signed four transformative tax reform bills into law, representing the most comprehensive overhaul of Nigeria's tax system since 1999. These reforms consolidate over a dozen existing tax laws into a unified framework and introduce significant changes across all tax categories.

### The Four Reform Acts:
1. **Nigeria Tax Act (NTA)** - Unified fiscal legislation for income, transactions, and instruments
2. **Nigeria Tax Administration Act (NTAA)** - Framework for fair and efficient tax administration
3. **Nigeria Revenue Service Act (NRSA)** - Establishes the Nigeria Revenue Service (replacing FIRS from January 2026)
4. **Joint Revenue Board Act (JRBA)** - Establishes coordination framework for all revenue agencies

## PART 1: COMPANIES INCOME TAX (CIT)

### 1.1 Corporate Tax Rate
- **Standard Rate:** 30% (retained, not reduced to 25% as initially proposed)
- **Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

### 1.2 Small Company Classification & Exemptions

**NEW DEFINITION OF SMALL COMPANIES:**
- Annual gross turnover: ≤ NGN 100 million (increased from NGN 25 million)
- Total fixed assets: ≤ NGN 250 million

**TAX EXEMPTIONS FOR SMALL COMPANIES:**
Small companies are now exempt from:
- Companies Income Tax (CIT)
- Capital Gains Tax (CGT)
- Development Levy

**Note:** Medium-sized company classification has been eliminated.

### 1.3 Development Levy

**NEW TAX:** 4% Development Levy on companies
- Applies to companies other than small companies
- Does NOT apply to companies in multinational groups
- Calculated on assessable profits
- Funds special development initiatives (TETFUND, NASENI, etc.)
- **Important:** This is a consolidation of pre-existing levies, not an entirely new charge

### 1.4 Minimum Effective Tax Rate

**PILLAR 2 IMPLEMENTATION:**
- Constituent entities of multinational enterprises must pay additional top-up tax if Effective Tax Rate (ETR) < 15%
- Aligns with OECD/G20 BEPS framework
- Implementation details to be specified in regulations
- Does NOT apply to Free Zone companies on export activities

### 1.5 Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) Rules

**NEW ANTI-AVOIDANCE PROVISIONS:**
- Nigerian companies must include foreign subsidiary profits in taxable income if:
  - Company controls foreign entity
  - Foreign subsidiary's tax rate < Nigeria's rate
  - Eliminates deferral advantage of parking profits in low-tax jurisdictions

## PART 2: PERSONAL INCOME TAX (PIT)

### 2.1 New Progressive Tax Structure
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026

**NEW TAX BANDS:**

| Annual Income Range | Tax Rate |
|---------------------|----------|
| ≤ NGN 800,000 | 0% (Full exemption) |
| NGN 800,001 - NGN 1,500,000 | 15% |
| NGN 1,500,001 - NGN 3,000,000 | 18% |
| NGN 3,000,001 - NGN 5,000,000 | 21% |
| NGN 5,000,001 - NGN 10,000,000 | 23% |
| Above NGN 10,000,000 | 25% |

**KEY CHANGES:**
- Full tax exemption for minimum wage earners (≤ NGN 800,000 annually)
- Lower rates for middle-income earners
- Higher rates for high-income earners
- More progressive structure overall

### 2.2 Definition of Resident Individuals

**WORLDWIDE INCOME TAXATION:**
Resident individuals are now taxed on worldwide income. A person is considered resident if:
- They are present in Nigeria for 183 days or more in a 12-month period, OR
- They have substantial economic ties in Nigeria, OR
- Their immediate family resides in Nigeria during the year of assessment

**NON-RESIDENT TAXATION:**
- Employment income taxable only if:
  - Duties performed in Nigeria, AND
  - No tax paid on such income in country of residence

### 2.3 Rent Relief

**NEW RELIEF ALLOWANCE:**
- Consolidated Relief Allowance (CRA) eliminated
- New rent relief: 20% of annual rent paid
- Maximum: NGN 500,000 (whichever is lower)
- Must provide proof of rent payment to claim

### 2.4 PAYE Compliance

**EMPLOYER OBLIGATIONS:**
- Uniform monthly withholding required
- Must remit PAYE on time
- Standardized documentation of employee benefits
- Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS) has enforcement powers nationwide
- Penalties for non-compliance

## PART 3: VALUE ADDED TAX (VAT)

### 3.1 VAT Rate
**CURRENT RATE:** 7.5% (retained, not increased as initially proposed)

### 3.2 Zero-Rated Goods and Services

**EXPANDED LIST OF ZERO-RATED ITEMS:**
- Basic food items
- Medical and pharmaceutical products
- Educational books and materials
- Electricity generation and transmission services
- Medical equipment and services
- Tuition fees
- Exports (excluding oil and gas exports)
- Agricultural products
- Educational services

**SIGNIFICANCE:**
- Businesses selling zero-rated items can recover input VAT costs
- Previously, zero-rating prevented VAT recovery

### 3.3 Input VAT Recovery

**GLOBAL BEST PRACTICE ADOPTION:**
- Businesses can claim input VAT on ALL purchases
- Includes services and fixed assets (previously excluded)
- Input VAT must be directly related to taxable supplies
- Proper documentation required
- Significant cost reduction for service-based businesses

### 3.4 VAT Administration & Technology

**MANDATORY DIGITAL COMPLIANCE:**
- **VAT Fiscalization:** Real-time transaction reporting to tax authorities
- **E-Invoicing:** Electronic invoice generation and submission required
- **National Single Window:** Unified platform for tax compliance
- Implementation phased approach
- Non-compliance penalties apply

## PART 4: CAPITAL GAINS TAX (CGT)

### 4.1 New Tax Rates

**COMPANIES:**
- Previous rate: 10%
- **New rate: 30%** (aligned with CIT rate)
- Eliminates tax arbitrage between capital gains and trading income

**INDIVIDUALS:**
- Taxed at applicable personal income tax rate (based on income bracket)
- Progressive rates from 0% to 25%

### 4.2 Computation Method

**CHARGEABLE GAINS:**
- Computed as difference between net proceeds and tax written down value
- Must maintain proper asset records
- Documentation of acquisition cost essential

**EXEMPTIONS:**
- Small companies remain exempt from CGT
- Certain government securities may be exempt
- Specific exemptions in legislation

## PART 5: PARTNERSHIP TAXATION

### 5.1 Partnership Income Taxation

**GENERAL RULES:**
- Where two or more companies carry out business in partnership, joint venture, or similar arrangement
- Income/profit constitutes a source of profits
- Each company's share is determined by relevant tax authority
- Partnership must register with relevant tax authority

### 5.2 Partnership Registration Requirements

**MANDATORY REGISTRATION:**
- Must register certified copy of partnership deed
- If no written deed, register particulars of oral/written agreement
- Registration required within 30 days of partnership formation
- Any changes to agreement must be registered within 30 days

### 5.3 Profit Determination

- Relevant tax authority determines each partner's profits/losses
- Where partner taxable in another jurisdiction, information shared between tax authorities
- Transparent information exchange mechanism

## PART 6: WITHHOLDING TAX (WHT)

### 6.1 New Withholding Tax Regulations 2024
**Effective Date:** January 2025 (already implemented)

### 6.2 Common WHT Rates

| Transaction Type | Rate |
|------------------|------|
| Dividends | 10% |
| Interest | 10% |
| Rent | 10% |
| Royalties | 10% |
| Professional Fees | 10% |
| Technical Services | 10% |
| Construction/Contracts | 5% |

**Note:** Rates may vary for specific circumstances; consult current regulations

### 6.3 WHT Administration

- Payer must withhold at source
- Remit to relevant tax authority within 21 days of payment
- Issue tax deduction certificate to payee
- Penalties for late remittance

## PART 7: TAX ADMINISTRATION & COMPLIANCE

### 7.1 Nigeria Revenue Service (NRS)

**TRANSITION FROM FIRS:**
- FIRS becomes NRS effective January 2026
- Expanded mandate and powers
- Enhanced technology infrastructure
- Improved taxpayer services

**POWERS & FUNCTIONS:**
- Assess, collect, and account for federal revenue
- Enforce tax compliance
- Conduct audits and investigations
- Issue tax clearance certificates
- Exchange information with foreign tax authorities

### 7.2 Joint Revenue Board (JRB)

**FORMERLY JOINT TAX BOARD:**
- Umbrella body for all revenue agencies in Nigeria
- Harmonizes taxes across all levels of government
- Ensures uniform application nationwide
- Coordinates federal, state, and local revenue authorities

**OBJECTIVES:**
- Standardize tax administration
- Resolve inter-jurisdictional disputes
- Share best practices
- Build capacity across revenue agencies

### 7.3 Tax Ombuds Office

**NEW INDEPENDENT OFFICE:**
- Serves as independent arbiter
- Reviews and resolves taxpayer complaints
- Liaises with tax authorities on behalf of taxpayers
- Handles disputes relating to taxes, levies, duties, or regulatory charges

**BENEFITS:**
- Provides alternative dispute resolution
- Reduces litigation
- Improves taxpayer confidence
- Faster resolution of issues

### 7.4 Digital Tax Administration

**MANDATORY TECHNOLOGY ADOPTION:**

**E-Filing:**
- All tax returns must be filed electronically
- Online payment systems mandatory
- Digital record-keeping required

**VAT Fiscalization:**
- Real-time sales transaction reporting
- Electronic fiscal devices (EFDs) required for VAT-registered businesses
- Automatic transmission of transaction data to NRS

**E-Invoicing:**
- Standardized electronic invoice format
- Must use NRS-approved invoicing systems
- Facilitates VAT tracking and compliance

**National Single Window:**
- Unified platform for all tax obligations
- One-stop access for taxpayers
- Integrated with other government systems

### 7.5 Transfer Pricing & International Taxation

**ENHANCED PROVISIONS:**
- Strict arm's length principle enforcement
- Transfer pricing documentation requirements
- Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) for multinational enterprises
- BEPS Action Plan implementation
- Information exchange with treaty partners

### 9.1 Pioneer Status Incentive

**RESTRUCTURED FRAMEWORK:**
- Subject to CIT at 30% during pioneer period
- Capital allowances deferred until post-pioneer period
- Tertiary Education Tax applies
- Subject to Withholding Tax
- Development Levy implications (check specific provisions)

### 9.2 Free Trade Zone Incentives

**MAINTAINED BENEFITS:**
- 0% CIT on export income
- Minimum effective tax rule does NOT apply to exports
- Must be exclusively export-oriented
- Proper documentation required

### 9.3 Small Company Benefits

**COMPREHENSIVE EXEMPTIONS:**
- 0% CIT
- 0% CGT
- No Development Levy
- Simplified compliance requirements
- Must meet both turnover AND fixed asset thresholds

## PART 10: TAX COMPLIANCE PROCEDURES

### 10.1 Tax Registration

**MANDATORY REGISTRATION:**
- All taxable persons must register with NRS
- Tax Identification Number (TIN) required
- Online registration available
- Update changes within 30 days

### 10.2 Filing Returns

**RETURN TYPES & DEADLINES:**

**Companies:**
- Annual CIT return: Within 6 months of financial year-end
- Monthly VAT return: By 21st day of following month
- WHT returns: Within 21 days of transaction
- PAYE returns: Monthly

**Individuals:**
- Annual return: By March 31 of following year (for calendar year taxpayers)
- Self-employed: Within 90 days of year-end

**E-FILING MANDATORY:**
- Use NRS online platforms
- Digital signatures accepted
- Immediate confirmation receipt
- Track status online

### 10.3 Payment Methods

**APPROVED CHANNELS:**
- Online payment platforms
- Bank transfers (to designated accounts)
- Electronic payment systems
- Payment reference numbers must be obtained

**DEADLINES:**
- Payment due with filing of returns
- Quarterly installments for CIT
- Monthly for PAYE and VAT

### 10.4 Record Keeping

**REQUIREMENTS:**
- Minimum 6 years retention
- Electronic records acceptable
- Must be accessible for audit
- Includes: invoices, receipts, contracts, bank statements, accounting records

### 10.5 Penalties & Interest

**LATE FILING:**
- First month: NGN 25,000
- Subsequent months: Additional NGN 5,000 per month
- Maximum penalties apply

**LATE PAYMENT:**
- Interest: 10% per annum on unpaid tax
- Additional penalties for deliberate evasion
- Criminal prosecution for serious offenses

**NON-COMPLIANCE:**
- Failure to register: Penalties apply
- Failure to issue receipts/invoices: Sanctions
- Obstruction of tax officials: Criminal offense

- Prevents double taxation

### 13.2 Permanent Establishment (PE)

**DEFINITION:**
- Fixed place of business through which enterprise wholly/partly carries on business

**TAX IMPLICATIONS:**
- Foreign company with PE in Nigeria subject to CIT
- Must register and file returns
- Separate accounting for PE required

- Prevents profit shifting through excessive debt

## PART 15: TAX PLANNING & STRATEGY

### 15.1 Legitimate Tax Planning

**ACCEPTABLE PRACTICES:**
- Utilizing available incentives
- Timing of income and expenses
- Choice of business structure
- Investment in tax-exempt securities
- Group restructuring (genuine commercial reasons)

### 15.2 Unacceptable Practices (Tax Avoidance)

**PROHIBITED SCHEMES:**
- Artificial transactions with no commercial substance
- Transfer pricing manipulation
- Treaty shopping
- Profit shifting to low-tax jurisdictions
- Round-tripping

**CONSEQUENCES:**
- Assessments raised
- Penalties imposed
- Criminal prosecution (in serious cases)
- Naming and shaming

### 15.3 General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR)

**APPLICATION:**
- Transactions lacking commercial substance void for tax purposes
- NRS can disregard transactions designed to avoid tax
- Burden of proof on taxpayer to show commercial purpose

## PART 16: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS (FAQs)

### Q1: When do the new tax laws take effect?
**A:** January 1, 2026 for most provisions. Some provisions took effect upon signing (June 26, 2025).

### Q2: What is the current VAT rate?
**A:** 7.5% (retained, not increased)

### Q3: What is the corporate income tax rate?
**A:** 30% (retained, not reduced to 25%)

### Q4: Who qualifies as a small company?
**A:** Companies with annual turnover ≤ NGN 100 million AND total fixed assets ≤ NGN 250 million.

### Q5: Are small companies exempt from all taxes?
**A:** Small companies are exempt from CIT, CGT, and Development Levy. They must still pay VAT (if registered), PAYE, WHT, and other applicable levies.

### Q6: Can I claim VAT on services now?
**A:** Yes, input VAT on services and fixed assets can now be claimed if related to taxable supplies.

### Q7: Does France have access to Nigerian taxpayer data?
**A:** No. The MoU is for technical assistance and capacity building only. No taxpayer data is shared.

### Q8: When does FIRS become NRS?
**A:** January 2026.

### Q9: What is the minimum wage exemption threshold?
**A:** NGN 800,000 annual income (fully exempt from PIT).

### Q10: How is rent relief calculated?
**A:** 20% of annual rent paid, maximum NGN 500,000 (whichever is lower).

### Q11: What is the Development Levy?
**A:** 4% levy on assessable profits of companies (excluding small companies and certain exempt entities). It consolidates previous levies.

### Q12: Must I file returns electronically?
**A:** Yes, e-filing is mandatory.

### Q13: What is VAT Fiscalization?
**A:** Real-time reporting of sales transactions to tax authorities using approved electronic devices.

### Q14: Can I appeal a tax assessment?
**A:** Yes, you can object to the assessment, then appeal to the Tax Appeal Tribunal if unsatisfied.

### Q15: What records must I keep?
**A:** All financial records, invoices, receipts, contracts, and accounting books for minimum 6 years.

## PART 19: GLOSSARY OF TAX TERMS

**Assessable Profits:** Total profits on which tax is computed after allowable deductions

**Arm's Length Principle:** Principle that transactions between related parties should be priced as if between independent parties

**BEPS:** Base Erosion and Profit Shifting - tax avoidance strategies

**Capital Allowances:** Tax deductions for capital expenditure on qualifying assets

**Chargeable Gains:** Gains subject to capital gains tax

**CIT:** Companies Income Tax

**CGT:** Capital Gains Tax

**Controlled Foreign Company (CFC):** Foreign subsidiary controlled by Nigerian parent

**Development Levy:** 4% levy on company profits for development purposes

**DGFIP:** Direction Générale des Finances Publiques (France's tax administration)

**E-Filing:** Electronic filing of tax returns

**E-Invoicing:** Electronic generation and submission of invoices

**ETR:** Effective Tax Rate

**FIRS:** Federal Inland Revenue Service (becomes NRS in 2026)

**Fiscalization:** Real-time transaction reporting to tax authorities

**GAAR:** General Anti-Avoidance Rules

**Input VAT:** VAT paid on purchases that can be offset against output VAT

**Joint Revenue Board (JRB):** Coordinating body for all Nigerian revenue agencies

**MoU:** Memorandum of Understanding

**NRS:** Nigeria Revenue Service (from January 2026)

**NTAA:** Nigeria Tax Administration Act

**NTA:** Nigeria Tax Act

**Output VAT:** VAT collected on sales

**PAYE:** Pay As You Earn (income tax withholding on employment income)

**PE:** Permanent Establishment

**PIT:** Personal Income Tax

**Tax Ombuds:** Independent office for resolving tax disputes

**TIN:** Tax Identification Number

**Transfer Pricing:** Pricing of transactions between related entities

**VAT:** Value Added Tax

**WHT:** Withholding Tax

**Zero-Rated:** Taxed at 0% but allows input VAT recovery
